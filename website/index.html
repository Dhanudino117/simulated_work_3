Problem Statement

Municipal corporations collect household water consumption data but lack visibility into usage inefficiencies. This analysis identifies abnormal consumption patterns, high-usage neighborhoods, and seasonal demand trends to inform conservation strategies.

Visualizations

📈 Monthly Water Usage Trend

Average water consumption across all households per month. The chart clearly shows a peak during summer months (June–August), driven by increased outdoor and cooling usage.

🏘️ Neighborhood Comparison

Tech Hub and Market Square consume significantly more water on average, likely due to larger household sizes and commercial activity. Green Park and Old Town are the most efficient neighborhoods.

🌤️ Seasonal Usage Distribution

Boxplots reveal that summer usage is both higher and more variable. The wider spread indicates inconsistent usage behavior — some households conserve while others spike.

🚨 Anomaly Detection

Red markers indicate households with abnormally high usage (beyond mean + 2 standard deviations). These likely indicate leaks, meter faults, or unreported commercial use.

🗺️ Neighborhood × Month Heatmap

This heatmap shows per-person monthly usage across all neighborhood–month combinations. Darker red cells are priority targets for conservation outreach.

Key Insights

  • 🏆
    Tech Hub is the highest-consuming neighborhood Average usage is ~40% above the city-wide mean. Likely driven by larger household sizes and potential commercial mixing in residential data.
  • ☀️
    Summer usage is ~40% higher than winter June, July, and August consistently show the highest demand. Conservation campaigns are most impactful if launched in May before the spike.
  • ⚠️
    ~3% of readings are anomalous Around 15 records show consumption 2–4x the normal range. These should be physically inspected for leaks or meter malfunctions.
  • 👤
    Per-person usage reveals true inefficiency When normalised by household size, some "low-total" neighborhoods actually have higher per-person usage, masking inefficiency.
